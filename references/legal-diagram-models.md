# Legal diagram models

Read only the section that matches the requested proceeding. Combine models when the matter crosses procedural stages.

## Civil litigation

Useful lanes: claimant/plaintiff, defendant, court, third parties, enforcement body.

Common stages:

1. pre-action notice, demand, preservation, limitation check;
2. filing and jurisdiction/venue;
3. service and proof of service;
4. appearance, answer, motion, counterclaim, default;
5. pleadings amendment and joinder;
6. disclosure/discovery and privilege;
7. interim relief and dispositive motions;
8. pretrial conference and settlement;
9. trial, burden, verdict, judgment;
10. post-judgment motions, appeal, stay, enforcement.

Branch explicitly for service failure, jurisdictional dismissal, default, settlement, summary disposition, remand, and appeal.

## Criminal procedure

Useful lanes: investigator/police, prosecutor, accused/defense, court, corrections or supervisory body.

Keep coercive state actions visually distinct from prosecutorial decisions and judicial authorization. Include custody, charging, first appearance, bail, disclosure, plea, trial, sentence, appeal, collateral review, and supervision only when within scope. Mark standards such as probable cause, reasonable suspicion, or beyond reasonable doubt only when supported by the governing jurisdiction.

## Administrative and regulatory proceedings

Useful lanes: regulated party, investigating staff, deciding official/tribunal, reviewing court, affected third party.

Separate investigation, notice, opportunity to respond, hearing, initial decision, internal review, final agency action, judicial review, compliance, and penalties. Identify whether exhaustion is mandatory and whether review stays the decision.

## Arbitration and private dispute systems

Useful lanes: claimant, respondent, administering institution, tribunal, court support/enforcement.

Show the consent or clause that creates jurisdiction. Separate institutional administration from tribunal decisions and court powers. Typical stages include notice, appointment, jurisdictional objections, procedural conference, written submissions, evidence/hearing, award, correction, set-aside, recognition, and enforcement.

For industry or professional self-regulation, distinguish recommendation, publication, membership consequence, platform report, and referral from legally binding government enforcement.

## Appeal and review

Useful lanes: appellant, appellee/respondent, lower tribunal, appellate body.

Include:

- appeal as of right versus permission/leave;
- notice and record preparation;
- cross-appeal;
- briefing sequence;
- standard and scope of review;
- new evidence restrictions;
- oral argument;
- affirm, reverse, modify, vacate, dismiss, or remand;
- mandate/remittitur and further review;
- stay and enforcement during appeal.

Do not merge a request for leave with a perfected appeal.

## Enforcement, execution, and compliance

Useful lanes: prevailing party/regulator, obligated party, enforcement court/agency, third-party holder.

Start from an enforceable instrument: judgment, order, award, undertaking, consent decree, or recommendation. Then show notice, voluntary compliance period, status report, noncompliance finding, sanctions or referral, execution methods, challenge/exemption, satisfaction, and closure.

## Evidence and burden map

Use an issue tree or directed evidence graph rather than a chronological flowchart.

For each claim, defense, or element, connect:

- legal element;
- burden holder;
- standard of proof;
- supporting evidence;
- opposing evidence;
- admissibility or privilege issue;
- unresolved factual gap;
- consequence if not proved.

Do not imply that the number of exhibits determines weight.

## Limitation and deadline timeline

Use a true time axis when sequence and elapsed time are the main question. Mark accrual, discovery rule, notice, filing, service, tolling start/end, extension, judgment, appeal notice, and long-stop date. Put disputed dates in a separate uncertainty band instead of choosing one silently.
