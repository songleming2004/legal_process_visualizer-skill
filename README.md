# Legal Process Visualizer

[English](README.md) | [简体中文](README.zh-CN.md)

Turn dense judgments, procedural rules, case files, and legal relationships into clear, editable visual maps.

Legal Process Visualizer is a Codex skill for work that is difficult to understand from prose alone. It identifies the people and institutions involved, places events in order, separates the main route from exceptions and appeals, shows who acts toward whom, and keeps the supporting authority visible in the result.

## What it can create

- **Procedural flowcharts** showing applications, hearings, decisions, service, appeal, enforcement, and alternative routes.
- **Litigation timelines** combining events from several courts or jurisdictions without losing their chronological relationship.
- **Decision trees** showing how different facts or rulings lead to different procedural outcomes.
- **Legal-relationship diagrams** showing ownership, control, agency, trust, transfer, responsibility, remedies, and changes over time.
- **Deadline maps** connecting each time limit to the event that starts it.
- **Coordinated diagram sets** when one page would be too crowded—for example, a main proceeding, an appeal path, and a relationship map.
- **Revisions of existing diagrams** to improve structure, citations, readability, or ambiguous arrows.

The usual output is an editable SVG, so names, wording, colors, positions, and connections can be revised later.

## What makes it useful for legal work

- It separates binding rules from judicial interpretation, practice observations, and explanatory notes.
- It places the relevant provision or judgment paragraph next to the step it supports.
- It distinguishes mandatory acts from discretionary choices and fixed deadlines from periods that the source does not define.
- It makes exceptions, dismissal routes, remand, appeal, enforcement, and other easily missed outcomes visible.
- It checks whether each arrow clearly connects the intended subjects, so a relationship is not left pointing into empty space or toward the wrong heading.
- It can work from a limited source set, such as one judgment only, or from a broader set of governing materials when requested.

## Examples

### 1. Turn one judgment into three coordinated diagrams

**Request**

> Use only the attached judgment. Create three editable diagrams: a cross-jurisdiction case timeline, the court's procedural and reasoning path, and a relationship map showing the share transfers and remedies. Put the relevant judgment paragraphs in each diagram.

**Result**

A coordinated set that answers three different questions: what happened and when, how the court reached its result, and how the parties, property, transfers, and remedies relate to one another.

### 2. Map a complete procedure from rules

**Request**

> Turn these arbitration rules into a detailed procedural flowchart from commencement through the final award. Show notice, tribunal formation, challenges, interim measures, evidence, hearings, correction of the award, and every stated deadline.

**Result**

A source-cited procedure map in which each deadline is tied to its triggering event and optional or exceptional routes are visibly separated from the ordinary route.

### 3. Explain an appeal route

**Request**

> Create a decision tree showing whether permission to appeal is required, who may grant it, the filing deadline, possible interim relief, and the outcomes of affirmance, variation, remand, and dismissal.

**Result**

A reader can start from the decision below and follow the applicable branch to the available appellate outcome without searching across several provisions.

### 4. Show a changing ownership and control structure

**Request**

> Draw a legal-relationship diagram showing the trust, trustee, company, beneficial owner, nominees, transferred shares, and the court-ordered restoration. Show when each relationship began or ended and distinguish historical from current ownership.

**Result**

A directional relationship map that identifies who held, controlled, transferred, or was required to restore each asset, including changes over time.

### 5. Repair an existing legal diagram

**Request**

> Review this editable SVG. Keep its visual style, but fix ambiguous arrows, crowded citations, missing branches, and any node whose wording overstates the source.

**Result**

A revised diagram that preserves the original design while making the legal relationships and source boundaries easier to audit.

## How to get a better result

When possible, include:

- the jurisdiction and forum;
- the source materials to use;
- whether the source scope is limited, such as “use only this judgment”;
- the desired start and end points;
- whether you want one diagram or several coordinated diagrams;
- the intended reader and preferred level of detail;
- whether the final visual must remain editable.

If these choices are not supplied, the skill will resolve only the points that materially change the result.

## Scope

The skill supports legal research organization and visual communication. It does not replace jurisdiction-specific legal advice. Any unavailable authority, disputed deadline, inference, or intentionally omitted procedural track should remain visible as a limitation rather than being silently filled in.
